---
title: Theme and glass pipeline
description: rikka-theme resolves layered and generated themes; rikka-blur declares glass regions the compositor renders.
about: crates/graphics/rikka-theme
tags: [theme, glass]
---

- `ThemeSpec` (TOML: named colours, variant, opacity, optional lens) derives an immutable `Theme`.
- `ThemeRegistry::discover(material)` layers built-in, generated, system and user themes by slug. The most specific wins.
- rikka-theme reads no settings. `ThemeRegistry::named(slug, material)` gives the spec a setting names: empty is the built-in default, anything else goes through `ThemeRegistry::resolve`, which answers one slug as `discover` would (theme folders most specific first, then only the one generated Material spec, then built-ins); unknown or unreadable falls back to the default with a warning.
- The house themes are the mascot's: `themes/rikka-light.toml` (`Theme::LIGHT_SLUG`, `Theme::light()`, the default) and `themes/rikka.toml` (`Theme::DARK_SLUG`, `Theme::dark()`). They are Nord hues on Material 3 tones: neutral hue 270, ice blue accent hue 244, hue slots at tone 40 (light) or 80 (dark). There is no hard-coded palette; every theme is a spec.
- A Material source colour, style and contrast generate Material You (dark and light) as plain specs. The colour science is the internal `rikka-material-color` (`Scheme::new(source, Variant, dark, contrast)` with the twelve roles the themes read, `Hct`, `TonalPalette`, `harmonize`).
- The background is its own axis, not a theme: `ThemeSpec::glass()` and `ThemeSpec::liquid()` turn any spec translucent, keeping a clearer opacity or a lens the theme declares. The setting is `rikka.appearance/background` (`appearance::Background`: solid, glass, liquid; one of `GLASS_KEYS`).
- `crates/session/rikka-appearance` is the only reader of the session appearance settings (Linux, `#[cfg(unix)]`): `Appearance::read(store)|current()` gives slug, Material You, background and glaze, `with_theme(slug)`, `registry()`, `theme()`, and re-derives only when a relevant setting changed. `ResolvedAppearance` holds an `Appearance` over mirrored `Settings` and answers each seam `Delivery` (`apply_delivery`) with the next theme. rikka-app, the shell and the compositor use it.
- On Windows (`#[cfg(windows)]`) `Appearance` reads the system instead and always gives the opaque WinUI 3 theme (`rikka_theme::winui`); slugs, Material You, custom themes, system backdrops and `ResolvedAppearance` do not exist there. API: `Appearance::current()` (system dark/light, accent ramp, high contrast), `theme()`, `is_dark()` (for `DWMWA_USE_IMMERSIVE_DARK_MODE`), `PartialEq` to skip unchanged re-reads. `unsafe fn rikka_appearance::is_appearance_change(message, wparam, lparam) -> bool` (window-procedure parameters) is true for `WM_SETTINGCHANGE` with lParam `"ImmersiveColorSet"` (dark/light, accent) or wParam `SPI_SETHIGHCONTRAST`, `WM_DWMCOLORIZATIONCOLORCHANGED`, `WM_SYSCOLORCHANGE` and `WM_THEMECHANGED`; on true the app calls `Appearance::current()` again.
- `rikka_theme::winui` (platform-independent, tested on Linux) builds the Windows themes: `winui::theme(Variant, &AccentPalette)` maps the published WinUI 3 tokens (`winui/tokens.rs`, named after the XAML keys) onto `Palette` (base fill → background, card fill → surface, ControlAltFillColorQuarternary → surface variant, text fills composited over the base, SurfaceStrokeColorDefault → outline, system fills → danger/warning/success, accent fill AccentDark1 light / AccentLight2 dark); selection, hues (harmonized like Material You, `hues.rs`), ANSI, syntax and interaction layers come from the central derivation through `Theme::assemble`. `winui::high_contrast(&SystemColors)` wears the GetSysColor colours. `AccentPalette` holds Accent and Light1-3/Dark1-3 (`from_registry` reads `Explorer\Accent\AccentPalette`, `from_accent` derives a ramp, `WINDOWS_DEFAULT`); `winui::from_colorref` reads COLORREF/DWM ABGR values.
- `Palette` is `Copy`. Semantic foregrounds are re-derived for WCAG AA after fills change. Selection has its own fill and foreground roles.
- `Corner` (re-exported by rikka-ui and `rikka_app::prelude`) is the one radius scale: `Window` 16, `Panel` 14 (cards, menus, popovers, sheets), `Control` 10, `Item` 8 (rows, chips, thumbnails), `Detail` 4. `Corner::nested(inset)` gives the concentric radius of a shape inset inside another. Code names a token, never a number; `ControlSize::radius()` maps Small to `Item` and the rest to `Control`.
- `rikka_theme::caption` holds the window caption metrics (bar height, separator, button width, glyph, title inset and size, resize border and corner) shared by rikka-widgets' title bar and the compositor's server-side captions.
- Derived centrally: tone steps, interaction layers, typography, ANSI colours, and `Syntax` (one colour per `Token` from theme hues).
- Only the colour picker hard-codes colours. Theme changes cross-fade with the shared motion tokens.
- User theme errors go to `LoadReport`. A broken embedded theme is a developer error.
- `Translucency` combines theme opacity with session opacity and an optional `Lens`. Ink stays opaque.
- Glass drops solid grounds for a translucent accent rim. Liquid adds lens refraction, which is active only with blur.
- `rikka-blur` (session layer) is the `ext-background-effect-v1` client: `Glass::bind(host)` (no roundtrip; the manager's capabilities arrive later) then `for_surface(surface)` gives a `BlurredSurface` that sends rounded regions per commit (`set_region`) once the compositor has announced BLUR. The compositor always announces BLUR; the session's blur setting only decides whether it draws ordinary glass. `SurfaceRegions` holds a surface's blur (`set_blur`), takes the frame's panes (`set_regions`) and applies them at present time (`apply`), never during layout.
- Opaque regions: widgets call `PaintCtx::opaque(shape)` where they paint fully opaque (the window frame when its background alpha is 1); `Ui::opaque()` collects them in logical px (an opaque scene background counts as the whole surface; anything under a fading ancestor is dropped). `SurfaceRegions::with_opaque_region(OpaqueRegions::bind(host)?.for_surface(surface))` declares `wl_surface.set_opaque_region` with `opaque_rectangles`: shape interiors minus each corner's `CORNER_REACH` square, minus blurred panes. rikka-app windows opt in unless created transparent; glass themes declare nothing. `Theme::opaque_palette()` is the palette before translucency, `Theme::with_translucency(session)` combines the session's, `Theme::foreground_on(fill)` picks contrasting text.
- Scrims (full-area backdrops behind modal dialogs and lightboxes) never darken: they only blur, with the fixed `Translucency::SCRIM_BLUR`, whatever the session's background, blur toggle or radius. They never refract and never show hover, press or ripple feedback. In-window: the `rikka_widgets::scrim()` leaf (`.on_dismiss`, swallows presses and scrolls) or `PaintCtx::draw_scrim(rect)`; content on top uses theme inks. Shell surfaces: `widgets::glass_scrim` plus `SurfaceDesc::scrim()` (always binds blur), which names the layer `rikka_session::surfaces::SCRIM`; `glass_scrim` stops declaring its region while exiting. The compositor draws scrim layers with `Glass::scrim()` and fades their panes in and out (`animation::Fades` keyed by layer, `Staging::scrims` opacity, `Frosting::Scrim` keeps the last shapes while fading out), unless the scrim surface has a `wp_alpha_modifier_v1` (`Fader::Client`): then it shows at once and the client fades it.
- Glass and scrim panes behind a surface are multiplied by its `wp_alpha_modifier_v1` alpha, like its content (`glass/panes.rs`).
- `shell_kit::DesktopFrost` is a Bottom-layer scrim that frosts the whole desktop under the taskbar (overview, desk editor): `frost.surface(presence, exit)` keeps its region declared and fades only by alpha, the overlay's desc gets `.drives(frost.opacity())` and its paint calls `frost.set(output, spread)`, so the frost follows the spread frame for frame, swipes included.
- Blur objects drop before their `wl_surface`.
