- Entry: `Cloth::builder().gravity(v).rate(hz).floor(h).quality(rikka_physics_tailor::Quality::{Low,Medium,High}).build(&compute)`; `add_figure(Arc<ClothAsset>)` uses each `Piece.style`, `add_styled_figure(cloth asset, styles)` overrides; `set_style(figure, piece, style)` rebakes statics only.
- `advance(time, &Inputs::new().figure(id, &instance).wind(wind))` is one fixed step of 1/rate; a figure missing from the inputs holds its last posture. Palettes are `transform * worlds[node] * rest_worlds[node].inverse()` (worlds exclude the instance transform), decomposed and slerped for E+1 epoch boundaries.
- Fresh or reset figures warm up inside their first advance: positions start at `transform * rest`, then 5 rounds blend bind→pose and 45 settle, all fully carried, so modelled-out ribbons (a figure's tie) hang before frame 0. A pose jump re-dresses the same way: `inertia::jumped` fires when any piece frame centre moves faster than 20 m/s or its bone turns faster than 60 rad/s between steps (a figure snapping back to standing between postures); carrying the cloth through such a jump let the legs sweep through it and left the tie folded on itself for good.
- Grabs: `Inputs::grab(figure, Grab { id, pick: Arc<ParticlePick>, point, strength })` (`Grab` and `MAX_GRABS` live in rikka-physics-tailor) holds a tailor `ParticlePick` at a world point; points and strengths interpolate from the same id's previous step (`GrabState`, checkpointed, cleared by `reset`), warm-up rounds ramp them in. The solve's `grip` pass (after `settle`) pulls each member to point + (its target − the centre's target) with per-substep share from weight × strength, and runs again after `retidy` for fully held members only, so they stay on the point however far it goes (grabs never slip; the cloth stretches instead); `PieceRecord.held` loosens that piece's pull, reach and shape keeping. At most `MAX_GRABS` per figure.
- Outputs: `drapes(figure)` = per mesh node `Drape { node, vertices, buffer, ready }` in rikka-renderer-3d's 48-byte `RikkaDrapeVertex` (`drape.slang` declares the same layout as its own `DrapeVertex`), world space, unbound vertices zeroed; buffers come from a `Ring` and are never rewritten while held. `read_particles`, `checkpoint`/`restore` (bit-identical), `motion()` (max displacement speed after the last retidy, read `READBACK_LAG` steps late), `set_profiling`/`profile`, `set_tracing`/`trace()` (per pass FNV hashes of every figure's state, contacts, scratch and normals plus the grid cells, `Traced { round, epoch, pass, figure, buffer, hash }`, computed by `hash.slang` on the GPU).
- Batch-taking forms: `advance_into(batch, time, inputs)`, `show_into`, `restore_into` return a `Recorded` the caller `commit(recorded, &ticket)`s after submitting (that sets the drapes' `ready`); `capture_into(&self, batch)`; `checkpoint_into(batch) -> PendingCheckpoint<'_>` borrows the cloth until `finish()` after the submit. One batch may hold at most `READBACK_LAG` (4) consecutive advances, since a still step waits on the stats of the step four back. `recorded_drapes(figure)` yields the latest recorded drape buffers before landing (what the fluid drapes from).
- `capture(&self)` returns a `PendingCapture` (`finish()` waits its ticket, like the fluid's) and takes its buffer from the figure's `captures` `Ring` (mutex), so a bake recycles buffers its cache dropped. `Figure::new` rejects figures whose per-figure dispatches (`src/launch.rs` `Extent`: probe = particles × 16 / 64 groups, targets, skin_body, obstacles, wraps, drape) pass 65 535 groups, the limit every Vulkan device guarantees; the renderer does not expose the device's own limit.
- Determinism: `tests/schedules.rs` (declared from `tests/machine.rs`, RTX only) traces fold and dance three times, also with a far decoy figure that reshuffles workgroups and grid buckets, and names the first differing pass/buffer. Rules it enforced: a `sync()` between `settle` and the following phase (settle writes positions/starts.w others read), zero-weight ends never written in colour passes (pinned particles shared by one colour), probe top-K comparisons break every tie by id (early rejects included), new figure buffers are zero-filled.
- Never widen the solve workgroup past 256 threads: at 768 the NVIDIA driver (RTX 4060, 615.x) raised Xid 13 Graphics Exceptions in `solve_*` whenever another queue of the same device ran at the same time (rikka-animate's UI renderer; or a second `LiveCloth` on `compute.background()`), killing the channel so every later wait hung. A lone solve never faults, so only same-device concurrency reproduces it.
- Per-figure buffers: `posed` holds what every figure reads (epoch snapshot start then motion, contiguous so one copy fills both, and the skinned body at all boundaries); `scratch` holds only the figure's own working data (starts, preds, geometry, targets, slots, selfs, wraps, nearest), so probes and solves of different figures share one barrier interval. Every pass declares each buffer its shader touches through `JobRecord`/`FigureRecord` pointers; probe/solve read `posed` and `statics` of the round's figures only, and the solve declares `stats` only in the measured epoch.
- Shaders use the Vulkan memory model (`vulkan_memory_model = True` in `shaders/BUILD.bazel`). Data exchanged between invocations of one workgroup through buffers (positions, velocities, starts, slots, selfs, contacts, targets) uses the `Exchanged*` views (NonPrivatePointer accesses), shared memory goes through `shared_load`/`shared_store`, and `sync()` is `physics.memory`'s `exchange_barrier` (workgroup control barrier with MakeAvailable/MakeVisible); nothing crosses workgroups inside a dispatch (other groups and figures read the `posed` snapshot).
- One generic solve (`cloth/solve.slang`) serves three entry files: the `cloth.solve.positions` module declares `extern struct Positions : IPositions`, and `solve_wide`/`solve_narrow`/`solve_global.slang` each `export` it as the shared-memory SoA cache of 3840 or 2560 floats or the global store. Wide runs every group that fits it; narrow loads only on devices whose `workgroup_memory()` cannot hold wide, global mode otherwise. Measured per step (RTX 4060, dance sample, both layers): settle ≈ 0.5 ms, jacobi 0.15, retidy 0.15 (half in the last, limb-sided substep), stretch 0.08–0.1; barriers are cheap, per-thread latency chains are not. Each solve thread copies its `Job` record once (`job = pc.jobs[..]`); unrolling `held_by_body`'s candidate loops and 1024-thread groups were slower.
- Solver inputs never depend on readback timing (`src/readback.rs` `StatsReadback`): step n's `Rest` observes exactly step n−`READBACK_LAG`'s (n−4) measured `Movement` (`lagged`, blocking on that step's stats only, and only while the posture is still and the cloth awake; otherwise `drain` settles finished downloads without waiting; zero for steps 0–3 or after `reset`), resolved movements are kept by step index, and `Checkpoint` carries steps s−4..s−1 so `restore` continues bit-identically. The lag equals `LiveCloth`'s most steps per follow, so a viewport catching up never stalls on its own submit. Public `motion()` is the speed of the newest step ≤ n−4 after advance n (it lazily waits for that step's stats under a mutex, so the value never depends on timing); `read_particles` and `capture` never touch the stats queue. `tests/replay/pacing.rs` stops the figure mid-dance until asleep and needs bit-identical drapes and particles whether particles are read every step, steps pause with checkpoints, or a checkpoint is restored after a detour.
- Solver internals (layout colouring, per-step passes, contacts, wraps, rest, retidy, drape relief) are in the physics-cloth-solver note.
